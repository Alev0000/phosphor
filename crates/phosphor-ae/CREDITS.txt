PHOSPHOR - CREDITS
==================

Phosphor is an After Effects port of NTSCRT, made by alev. Almost everything
that makes it look good was made by the people below; Phosphor is the glue.

Vibecoded: the author is not a programmer. The code was written with AI,
then directed, tested and checked in After Effects by a human. It is shared
as-is; fixes and improvements are welcome.


THE ORIGINAL APP
----------------

NTSCRT by Finn McKenty - https://github.com/finnmckenty/NTSCRT
  The app Phosphor reproduces: its pipeline (Digital Video, NTSC, Downscale,
  Glitch, CRT, Screen Loop), its codec, Glitch receiver, video feedback,
  27 looks, 5 Screen Loop presets and default settings. Phosphor ports them
  from Swift/Metal to Rust/Direct3D 11.
  MIT license, Copyright (c) 2026 Finn McKenty. See LICENSES\NTSCRT-MIT.txt


THE SIGNAL
----------

ntsc-rs by valadaptive - https://github.com/ntsc-rs/ntsc-rs
  The NTSC/VHS signal, used unchanged (v0.9.4, the version NTSCRT uses).
  MIT OR ISC OR Apache-2.0. Phosphor's source is a fork of ntsc-rs, and its
  NTSC controls are adapted from ntsc-rs's own After Effects plugin.
  ntsc-rs is a port of ntscqt (JargeZ), a GUI for ntsc (zhuker), itself a
  port of composite-video-simulator (joncampbell123).


THE SCREEN
----------

librashader by SnowflakePowered (Ronny Chan)
  https://github.com/SnowflakePowered/librashader
  Runs the RetroArch shaders. Used unmodified under MPL-2.0
  (LICENSES\MPL-2.0.txt); its source is at the link.

libretro/slang-shaders - https://github.com/libretro/slang-shaders
  The CRT shaders, shipped as-is in "shaders" with their notices in each file.
  - crt-royale: TroggleMonkey (GPL-2.0-or-later; blur helpers MIT)
  - crt-easymode, crt-aperture: EasyMode (GPL)
  - crt-hyllian: Hyllian (MIT)
  - crtsim: J. Kyle Pittman (CC0)
  - crtglow: Themaister, moire mitigation by Timothy Lottes, added by hunterk
  GPL text: LICENSES\GPL-2.0.txt. The plugin loads them as separate files.


THE GLITCHES AND THE CODEC
--------------------------

analogtv by Trevor Blackwell, from XScreenSaver (Jamie Zawinski)
  The design NTSCRT's Glitch stage follows. NTSCRT's receiver (and so
  Phosphor's) is its own code, written from that design.

The Digital Video codec uses the reference tables of the JPEG standard
(ITU-T T.81, Annex K).
